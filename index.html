How this is ranked

For every matchup this week, the model projects a score (same math as Matchup — offense-vs-defense yardage, home-field advantage, turnover margin, and for CFB, a blend with CFBD's opponent-adjusted SP+ rating) and compares it to the actual market spread/total from the odds board. These are the games where that gap is biggest — where the model disagrees with the market the most, in either direction. Confidence tiers reflect edge size, and for CFB, whether SP+ data backed the projection (box-score-only projections are noisier early season). A big gap isn't automatically right; it just means the numbers loaded here see it very differently than the number your book is offering. Needs both team stats and a market line loaded to show up.

How this is ranked

Scans every loaded player with a game already logged and, for whoever's team is playing this week, finds the biggest mismatches between their production and this week's opponent's relevant defense — same defense-adjustment math as the hit rate checker. Players averaging only a token amount over their last 5 games (e.g. a fullback who rarely touches the ball) are filtered out — there's no depth-chart data in this pipeline to know who's a real starter, so recent production volume is the best available stand-in. Each remaining row includes a "projected" number: last-5-game average scaled by how much tougher or easier this opponent's defense is than league average. That's a simple heuristic, not a real sportsbook line — weigh it against whatever number your book is actually offering to decide which side of over/under looks like the better value.

How this works

Every week's bets get logged automatically before the games are played — every game's spread and total, not just a curated top 10, plus a player prop projection for every player who clears a minimum recent-volume bar. Once a game (or a player's next game) is final, its pick gets graded hit/miss/push against the real result. Team bets grade against the real market line. Player props have no real sportsbook line in this pipeline, so they grade against the model's own projected number — that measures whether the projection itself is well-calibrated, not whether a real bet would have won.
